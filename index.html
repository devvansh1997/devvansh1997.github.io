NeurIPS 2026 · Evaluations & Datasets Track

MaRiO: Multi-agent Collaborative Reasoning via Shared Observations in MLLMs

Nathaniel Redmond*, Fidel Omar Tito Cruz*, Devansh Sharma, Shehreen Azad, Sirshapan Mitra, Shruti Vyas, Yogesh S Rawat  (*equal contribution)

A benchmark of 3,255 questions over 1,680 multi-agent observation sets (real scenes plus VirtualHome, Habitat-Sim, and AI2-THOR) that tests whether multimodal LLMs can reason across agents' views with no shared camera information. Of 23 models evaluated zero-shot, the best (Gemini 2.5 Pro) reached 55.7% against 73.2% for humans; most open-source models scored 30–42%.

My role: co-designed the benchmark, ran data curation and annotation, built the annotation and review tool used by three expert annotators, and built the evaluation harness.

Under review · ICLR 2027

LLM Hallucination Detection via Low-rank Multilinear Decomposition

Devansh Sharma, Faizul Rakib Sayem, Shahana Ibrahim

LOTUS summarizes a large language model's hidden states with token order statistics and compresses them with an unsupervised Tucker-2 decomposition into 896-dimensional features for a hallucination classifier. It beat the supervised baseline HARP in all 8 model–dataset settings (+7.1 AUROC on average) and ACT-ViT in 6 of 8, with about 40× fewer projection parameters than PCA.

The paper also introduces within-question AUROC and question-level splits, which separate real hallucination detection from question-difficulty shortcuts, and shows that detectors transfer across LLMs with unlabeled alignment.

IEEE ITSC 2024

Comparison of Deep Object Detectors on a New Vulnerable Pedestrian Dataset

Devansh Sharma, Tihitina Hade, Qing Tian

Introduced and open-sourced BGVP, an object detection dataset of vulnerable pedestrians: 2,000 images with 5,932 manually annotated boxes in four classes (children, elderly, people with disabilities, non-vulnerable). Fine-tuned COCO-pretrained YOLOv4, YOLOv5, YOLOX, Faster R-CNN, and EfficientDet (best mAP@0.5 of 0.80); every detector was weakest on elderly pedestrians and in dark scenes.
